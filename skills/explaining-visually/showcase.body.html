The delivery drone turns back on its own when its battery cannot reach home

The drone keeps working out what the trip home costs, and starts a reserve return before the pilot has to react.

  • WhyPilots take 40 s on average to press “Home” after the warning; two drones have made emergency landings.

  • SizeFour steps, firmware and app; the server only forwards.

Today: the pilot is part of the loop

The decision to turn back is made by a person two network hops away. When the warning arrives late or the pilot is busy, the margin is already spent.

Reserve return picks the nearest of the base and approved landing sites, measured in energy, not metres: a headwind makes a short way expensive. From Delivering the same threshold applies, but the parcel is dropped first if it is under 30 s from delivery.

Home changes from a bare command into a state the drone can also enter on its own. The move to Reserve return is checked every second in both En route and Delivering.

When must it turn back?

Wind is squaredThe need grows with the square of the wind: doubling it from 7 to 14 m/s more than doubles the extra.

The way home goes over the buildings

The altitude comes from the map layer: the tallest building along the corridor plus 6 m, the rule pilots use today, now in firmware.

The estimator lives in the battery module, and Failsafe gains reserve return as a trigger next to a lost link.

The app and the message

The app only shows what the drone reports. Three new telemetry fields carry the decision and its reason, so the server forwards them unchanged.
